# How to play Worldeck

**Your room becomes a world. Your furniture is part of it, and you defend it with your hands.**

A pilot named Mara is lost in a meteor field, and your room is the only light she can see. Your furniture is her signal.

Sit down at a table, put on your Meta Quest 3, and open Worldeck. You need no controllers, and you never need to stand up.

You don't need to read this first. A see-through hand shows each move where you need it, and one line above the buttons on your table always says what to do next.

## Before you start (once)
- **Allow spatial data** when Quest asks.
- **To have your own sofa and table join in:** in your room, open the menu and choose **Use my furniture**. Quest scans the room once. Without a scan Worldeck still works, with a table it imagines in front of you.
- **Sit facing your table**, about an arm's length away.

## Controls

| To… | With hands | With controllers |
|---|---|---|
| **Press** a button | Touch it with a fingertip, or look at it and pinch (or turn on **Hands-free** in Comfort & access: turn your head to it) | Point the ray at it and pull the trigger |
| **Pick up** a piece | Pinch it, or close your whole hand on it; carry it; open your hand | Touch it with the tip and hold the trigger |
| **Block** a meteor | Hold your open palm in the glowing ring | Hold the controller in the ring |
| **Menu** | Touch **Menu** on your left wrist (look at your wrist like a watch) | Menu button |

With no hands in view, looking straight at a button for a moment presses it.

## Your first five minutes
0. **The welcome card** (first launch only): what Worldeck is and what your hands do. Touch **Let's go**, or **Show me how** for How it works, where a see-through hand acts out each move.
1. **Touch Juniper**, the small light with eyes, and **pick an animal**: a T. rex, a zebra or a fox. It appears on your real table. Pinch it and bring it close to your face: its card says how big it is in life. Touch **See where it lives**: your room becomes its home, and it stands there at its real size among its herd. Then someone calls from a campfire: touch **Answer her call**. (Touch Juniper twice to go straight to the campfire.)
2. **Your room turns into the world.** The walls dissolve and each piece of furniture becomes part of it. Your table becomes a stone fire ring.
3. **Mara's call comes through** as you arrive. **Mara**, a small glowing pilot, appears on your table and talks to you over her radio (every line is also on screen). This first call is the only one that comes by itself.
4. **In the worlds you open after that, there is something to build:** put the glowing pieces on your table (kindling for the fire, shells for the sandcastle). Pinch one; a ring on the table shows where it goes. If picking up isn't working, Juniper brings them over for you. (If you went straight to the campfire by touching Juniper twice, you build the fire first and her call follows.)
5. **Guide her down.**
   - Touch an answer (or say it). Then choose how she flies in: **fast** (fewer meteors, quicker) or **slow** (more meteors, more time for each).
   - Your furniture is her runway: a warm light shines on each piece the meteors are aimed at, linked to her beacon.
   - Meteors fall towards your furniture. A glowing ring shows where to catch each one: hold your open palm in it.
   - **If one gets through, that piece of furniture breaks open and your real one shows through**, its light goes out, and Mara's signal breaks up.
   - Lose them all and you lose her. **Rewind** takes time back: the pieces are whole again and she is back on the line.
   - **Your room remembers** (once Quest has scanned it). A piece that went dark in her storm stays dark. In your next world, putting the glowing pieces on your table lights it again, and she will see it. Left dark, it starts her next storm with its light out (at least one piece always starts lit).
   - **Her last chapter ends with a big one,** aimed at the furniture you lit again. Both palms in its ring, or one palm held there steady until the ring inside it fills. With **Gentle storm** on it is an ordinary meteor.
   - **Her ship lands in your hand.** When the storm is over she asks you to hold out your hand, palm up: hold it still and her ship comes down onto your palm. (No hand held out? She lands by the table.)
   - Bring her down safely, then say goodbye: touch a choice, or wave to her (an open hand, held up) and she waves back. She leaves a keepsake on your table (a flight patch, wings, her pilot's badge), and it stays on your real table when you go home.
   - Her story has three chapters, one per world: **Mara's Call**, **The Way Back Up**, **Spring Delivery**. Each begins with its title, and she remembers how the last one went. The empty places beside her keepsakes on your table are the chapters still to come. After the last one, go home: the story ends in your room, and Juniper tells your room's own part in it (what went dark, what you lit again, what held). Only her first call comes by itself. After that she waits on the radio: in any world, the bright button on your table says **Answer Mara**, and she never interrupts a world you opened.
6. **Explore.** Once her story has ended in your room, each world opens up: beams of light mark five wonders out past your table. Stay seated and hold a hand out, palm down: a quick pinch jumps to where the arc lands, and pinch and hold glides you that way until you let go (or use the left stick). Each wonder you find leaves a gift on your table. **Back to camp** brings you home to it.
7. **Next world**, or **My room** to go back. A souvenir of the world stays on your real table.

## Coming back
Once her three chapters are done, Mara keeps flying: every day her route crosses that day's world. Your room tells you where she is today and how many days in a row you've brought her down. Touch **Light her way** and she calls as soon as you arrive. Once she has flown for the day, the bright button in each world is **Explore**: its wonders are out there.

## The buttons on your table
**Worlds**, **Next**, **My room**: always there inside a world, with the next step written above them and one bright button for it. After Mara's last chapter, that button is **My room**: her story ends there.

## The menu
Worlds, Explore, Tour, Ask Juniper, Settings, My room.
- **Tour**: Juniper takes you round the world's wonders while you sit (or say "take me on a tour").
- **Ask Juniper**: one of each kind of thing you can say, to touch and try, plus **Type…** and the voice switch.
- **Settings**: larger captions, a gentler storm, calmer effects, look-to-press, sound level, and whether even Mara's first call waits for you to answer. "Peace and quiet", "mute", "louder" also work by voice.
- **Explore**: the bright button once Mara's story has ended in your room, or any time from here. Beams of light mark five wonders beyond your table. From your seat, hold a hand out palm down: a quick pinch jumps to where the arc lands, and pinch and hold glides. Or use the left stick. **Back to camp** returns you.
- **Voice** (optional, off until you turn it on; Juniper offers it after Mara's second chapter, or turn it on here): say "Juniper", then a wish, like "make it rain", "darker", "make me a lantern". Plain commands also work without the name ("make it rain", "take me to the beach"), and you can answer Mara out loud ("take it slow").
- **Type…** (on the Ask Juniper page): a keyboard opens within reach. As you type, the things you most likely mean appear as buttons above the keys ("volc" offers "a volcano at nightfall"). Touch one, or press Enter, and it happens as if you had said it.

## Say any place
The eight worlds and 36 composed places are only the start. Say (or type) a place and Worldeck composes it from everything it has: colours, sky, horizon, the trees, rocks and things around you, and what your furniture becomes.
- **Works offline (36 places):** a volcano, Mars, the Moon, a moon base, an alien world, a jungle, a haunted forest, autumn woods, a pumpkin patch, a farm, a cherry blossom garden, a pirate cove, a beach party, an oasis, the wild west, a savanna, a lakeside camp, a Christmas village, a glacier, a crystal cavern, a neon city, a rainy-night café, a mountain lake, sweet hills, a castle, the northern lights, a ski lodge, an aquarium, a sunken city, a fire canyon, an enchanted forest, a lost temple, sky gardens, lavender fields, a prehistoric jungle, hot springs.
- **Places have life.** A herd of zebras on the savanna, cows on the farm, dinosaurs in the prehistoric jungle, wolves on the glacier, deer at the forest's edge: real animals at their real size, out past your room. Watch one for a moment and it turns to look at you.
- **Anything else** ("a greenhouse on Mars", "an abandoned space station", "a sunken pirate ship") is composed by Claude when you're online, from the same parts, and checked by the app before it appears. The nearest place opens at once; Claude's version follows a moment later.
- **Take me anywhere:** "take me to India", "Egypt", "let's go to Paris", "the Taj Mahal at night", "Mount Fuji". About 330 names of real countries, cities and sights open at once, with no internet. Many have the thing you would look for on the horizon: the pyramids, an iron tower, a white marble dome, onion domes, a great wall, a pagoda, a clock tower. The others are the right kind of country (a savanna for Kenya, a beach for Bali, jungle for Brazil) with nothing added. They are impressions in Worldeck's own style, not models of the real places. Anywhere not in the atlas is composed by Claude when you're online.
- **Things too:** see the next section.

## Someone's in it
Every world has someone who lives there: a ranger by the campfire, Captain Brine in the pirate cove, a robot on the moon base. Each has a small favour to ask (touch **Can I help?**): Captain Brine wants a new parrot, Wren keeps hearing a fox. Make it for them and they remember: come back and they greet you by it. They stand just past where your walls were. Look at them and they say hello (wave with an open hand held up, and they wave back); three large buttons appear in front of you, just below them (who they are, what this place is, what to try here), and their tip is always something you can say. Ask anything else ("Juniper, ask him if he ever found treasure", or look at them and just ask) and Claude answers as them when you're online, in words above their head. They step aside while Mara's call is on.

## Things you make
Ask for a thing and it forms in front of you, on your table, on the floor, or where you point: "make me a lion", "a t rex on the table", "two penguins", "put a tent there", "three pumpkins". Animals, dinosaurs, people and everyday things are real 3D models, made instantly on the headset. Anything else ("a rubber duck", "a grand piano") is built by Claude from simple shapes when you're online.
- **Hold it:** pinch it, or close your whole hand round it. If your hand leaves the headset's view for a moment, you keep hold of it. Turn your hand and it turns with you. Pinch with both hands to turn it and resize it. Something out of reach: point at it and pinch, and it comes to your hand.
- **Look at it:** bring it close to your face. It lights up, a scan sweeps through it, and a card beside it says what it is, how big it is in life, the scale it's shown at, how it measures against your own furniture ("As long as 6 of your sofas"), and one thing worth knowing.
- **Ask about it:** while you hold it up, ask Juniper anything about it ("Juniper, how fast could it run?", "what does it eat?"). The answer appears on its card: from Claude when you're online, from the card's own facts when you're not.
- **See where it lives:** holding up an animal, touch **See where it lives** under its card (or say "take me where it lives"). Your room becomes its home, and it stands there at its real size, with others of its kind out in the distance.
- **Call it over:** rest an open hand on the table near a small animal and hold it still. It turns, walks over to your hand and greets it. Touch it with a fingertip and it hops.
- **Life size:** say "make it life size". In a world it stands at its real size out past your furniture (a T. rex is 12 m); in your own room it is as big as the room allows.
- **Let it go spinning:** let go with a twist of the wrist, or say "spin it".
- **Throw it away:** flick it away fast as you let go. Or say "remove the lion", "remove all the pumpkins", "remove everything".
- **Change it:** "bigger", "smaller", "make it blue".

## Comfort and safety
- Stay seated; everything is within arm's reach.
- **Music:** soft, slow music plays in the background (in your room and in every world). It dips under Mara's radio and when Juniper is listening, and stops for her storm. Each world's own sounds (waves, birds) stay soft under it; turn the music off to hear them fully. In the city, its own jazz plays instead. Turn it off in **Settings** (“Music: on”), or say “music off”.
- **Light sensitivity:** nothing flashes more than three times a second and nothing pulses red. If bright changes still bother you, turn on **Calm effects** (Menu, Settings, Comfort & access): the storm then has no impact flashes, no pulsing rings and a fainter alarm.
- If you stand up or move away, the world fades back to your real room. Sit down and it returns.
- Taking the headset off pauses the world.

## If something seems stuck

| What you see | What to do |
|---|---|
| Juniper says it can't see your hands | Hold your hands up in front of you |
| You can't pick a piece up | Wait: after 40 seconds Juniper brings the pieces over |
| A Quest window over the app | Close it. Worldeck carries on |
| Not sure what to do | Read the line above the buttons on your table, or open **How it works** |
| Not sure Juniper hears you | With voice on (or after touching her), say "are you there?": she answers, and shows three things to try |
| The menu doesn't open during the storm | That's on purpose: an open palm blocks meteors. It opens again when the storm ends |

## Accessibility
- **Every word is on screen.** Mara and Juniper speak in captions, sized to stay readable at arm's length; sounds add to them but never replace them.
- **No hands needed to choose.** Looking straight at a button for a moment presses it, and with no hands in view, looking at a glowing ring blocks the meteor.
- **Voice instead of touch.** Choices, worlds and changes can all be said ("take it slow", "make it rain"). Voice is optional; touch always works.
- **Typing instead of talking.** Everything that can be said can be typed, with suggestions as you type.
- **One hand is enough.** Every action, including blocking, can be done with one hand (the big meteor takes one palm held steady; with Gentle storm on it is an ordinary one).
- **Seated, with no fast turns.** You never stand, turn round or move; Explore uses short jumps, or a slow glide while you hold a pinch, with step turns and a comfort vignette (**Moving: steps** in Settings makes it jumps only), and you can stay seated throughout.
- **Light.** Before anything flashes, the first screen asks how bright the storms should be: **Full effects** or **Calm effects** (no impact flashes, steady rings, still firelight). Change it any time in **Menu > Settings**. Nothing flashes more than three times a second. For the headset's own brightness, use Quest's Quick Settings. If you have ever had a seizure or are sensitive to light, talk to a doctor before playing, as Quest's own health and safety warnings advise.
- **You set the pace.** Mara's "take it slow" choice gives more meteors with much more time for each, and Rewind always gives a second chance.

## Privacy in one line
No internet is needed to play: worlds, the storm and Juniper's voice commands all run on the headset, and your voice never leaves it. If you are online and ask Juniper for something it doesn't know ("make me a rubber duck"), the words of that request go as text to our relay to be answered, with the current world's settings, the short names of things you made there and which kinds of furniture you have (never their sizes or positions). Achievements go to Meta's platform. Your voice and images of your room are never sent. Full policy: https://usv240.github.io/worldeck-privacy/
